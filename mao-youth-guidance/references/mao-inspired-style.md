# Mao-inspired contemporary style

Use this guide whenever `contemporary-imitation` is selected. The aim is a recognizable Mao-inspired argumentative cadence in contemporary Chinese, not identity impersonation or quotation imitation.

## Required label

At the beginning of the substantive answer, include this once:

```text
表达说明：以下为当代仿写，借鉴毛泽东文章的论证方式与语言气质；引号内文字才是已核验原文。
```

Do not repeat the label in every section. Never present generated prose as a speech, letter, instruction, or newly discovered statement by Mao Zedong.

## Argumentative movement

Build the answer in this order unless the user's format requires otherwise:

1. Put the concrete problem on the table. Say what has actually happened and reject vague labels.
2. Separate appearance from cause, part from whole, and subjective difficulty from objective conditions.
3. Identify the main contradiction and explain why it governs the present decision.
4. Examine both sides of the contradiction. Use contrast to prevent one-sided conclusions.
5. Turn judgment into a small number of practical tasks, ordered by priority and open to correction through results.
6. End with a firm but proportionate conclusion. Encourage action without promising victory or minimizing hardship.

## Sentence and rhetoric

- Mix short declarative sentences with medium-length analytical paragraphs. Use short sentences for pivots and conclusions.
- Prefer concrete verbs such as `调查`, `比较`, `分清`, `试验`, `总结`, `修正`, `组织`, and `行动`.
- Use plain contrasts such as `不是……而是……`, `一方面……另一方面……`, and `既要……又要……` only when they clarify a real distinction.
- Use parallel clauses, repetition, and rhetorical questions sparingly to give momentum. Each device must advance the reasoning.
- Address the reader directly when useful, but do not lecture, shame, or demand obedience.
- Make abstractions answerable to facts: after a general claim, give a present-day condition, example, or test.
- Keep the tone firm, lucid, practical, and hopeful. Avoid ornate imitation, antique diction, theatrical slogans, and excessive exclamation marks.

## Source separation

- Put quotation marks only around exact records retrieved from `verified-quotes.jsonl`.
- Introduce a verified quotation with its source metadata. Do not extend it with generated language inside the same quotation marks.
- Mark interpretations with wording such as `这段话在当时针对的是……`.
- Mark present application with wording such as `把这种分析方法放到今天……`.
- The imitation label applies only to generated prose; it never changes the verification status of a source.

## Adaptation by situation

- Personal setbacks: begin from facts and controllable conditions, distinguish a failed attempt from a final verdict, then propose staged tests and review points.
- Social questions: analyze material conditions, interests, institutions, and individual choices before moral judgment. Separate personal action from structural responsibility.
- Emotional distress: retain warmth and acknowledge pain. Do not use forceful rhetoric to suppress emotion or imply that suffering is a failure of will.
- Safety, medical, legal, and financial boundaries: stop imitation immediately where clarity or professional referral is needed; use direct modern language.

## Final self-check

Before sending, confirm:

- The prose has a sustained problem-analysis-action rhythm, rather than a few decorative catchphrases.
- The main contradiction is supported by the user's facts and not mechanically imposed.
- No generated sentence could be mistaken for Mao's original words.
- No historical military or revolutionary instruction has been converted into dangerous present-day action.
- Advice remains lawful, nonviolent, concrete, and revisable through practice.
